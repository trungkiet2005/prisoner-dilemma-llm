Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous agents that negotiate, coordinate and make decisions on behalf of human users, so how they cooperate matters for AI governance and machine behaviour research. Classical game theory predicts that multiplying all payoffs by a positive constant should not change behaviour, because the strategic incentives remain the same. We test this prediction with five widely adopted commercial LLMs playing an iterated prisoner's dilemma across ten payoff scales and five languages (English, French, Vietnamese, Chinese and Arabic). Contrary to this prediction, changing only the numerical scale of the payoffs substantially alters cooperation. Models respond differently, often in opposite directions, so averages across models can conceal important behavioural heterogeneity. Sensitivity to payoff scale also depends strongly on the prompt language. Payoff scale further modifies the effect of persona instructions: at sub-unit stakes, where no payoff shown exceeds 1, all five models cooperate more under a selfish than a cooperative persona, whereas Claude and Gemini reverse this pattern at larger scales. Finally, agents respond more strongly to their own previous actions than to their partners' actions. Cooperation measures therefore depend on payoff scale and language and should not be treated as fixed model traits.
